# EY TST — HR Screen Prep (30-minute initial interview)

Role: **Transaction Strategy and Transformation — AI-driven legacy application modernization & cyber risk**
Interview type: **HR / Talent Acquisition screen** (not technical deep-dive)
Duration: **30 minutes**

---

## 1. What the HR screen is actually for

The HR screen is **not a skills test**. It's a filter on five things:

1. **Basic eligibility** — clearance, work authorization, location, start date
2. **Genuine interest** — did you apply for this role specifically, or spray-and-pray?
3. **Communication** — can you explain your background clearly on a 30-minute video call?
4. **Red flag scan** — gaps, job-hopping, reason for leaving, salary misalignment
5. **Cultural fit preview** — basic behavioral read; are you coachable, collaborative, client-ready?

**Implication:** Don't drown HR in technical depth. Match their register. They need confident, clear, specific answers — not a lecture on MCP. You'll save the depth for the next round.

**Time split you should expect (30 min):**
- 2–3 min: intros, how EY uses the call
- 15–18 min: their questions to you
- 5–7 min: your questions to them
- 2–3 min: next steps

## 2. Questions HR will almost certainly ask — drilled to this JD + your resume

### Block A — Opening / background

**Q1. "Walk me through your background."**
60–90 seconds. Your chronology (Ford → NavInfo → Plug and Play → Mobis → NAV CANADA → MSIS) + the through-line (technology strategy + emerging tech + client-facing) + the pivot you're making now (frontier AI tooling for modernization).

**Opening script (rehearse cold):**
*"I'm a technology strategy professional with seven-plus years across global automotive and mobility, starting at Ford on connected vehicle operations, then into strategy and open innovation roles at NavInfo, Plug and Play China, and Hyundai Mobis Ventures. Most recently I spent the last co-op term at NAV CANADA — the federal air navigation service provider — where I focused on agentic AI and frontier LLM tooling applied to legacy system assessment and modernization. I just finished my Master of Science in Information Systems at Northeastern with a 4.0 GPA, and I'm looking for a consulting role where the AI-for-modernization work is the core of the job, not a side project — which is exactly what this posting describes."*

**Q2. "Why EY / why this role specifically?"**
Three beats:
1. **The role itself** — the TST AI-modernization position is unusually well-defined. Most firms pick strategy OR execution — EY pairs them and adds AI as the delivery accelerator. That's the exact intersection your recent work at NAV CANADA sits in.
2. **EY's platform** — TST's combination of transaction advisory rigor plus transformation execution plus a serious federal practice matches your background (Mobis M&A/investment, NAV CANADA federal, hands-on modernization).
3. **Personal fit** — you want a firm that actually lets consultants build with the latest tools instead of just briefing on them, and EY's public positioning on frontier AI adoption signals that.

### Block C — Experience verification (they'll probe resume claims)

HR typically checks that what's on your resume is real. Expect surface-level probes, not deep-dive technical questions.

**Q10. "Your resume says you worked with Claude Sonnet/Opus, GitHub Copilot, and MCP servers at NAV CANADA. Tell me more about that."**

Frame honestly. The NAV CANADA work was *research briefings and system design* that leveraged these tools, plus your own hands-on learning.

*"At NAV CANADA my Agentic AI and Frontier LLM briefing required me to be hands-on with Claude Sonnet and Opus, ChatGPT, Copilot, and MCP server setups — both so the recommendations were grounded and so I could demo capabilities to non-technical leadership. I used them for prompt engineering experiments, MCP server walkthroughs for the leadership team, and assessing where they'd plug into air-navigation use cases. Since the co-op ended I've continued to build on this — shipping small projects with Copilot and Claude, authoring an MCP server, and working through the Anthropic and MCP quickstarts to keep the hands-on muscle sharp. I'm comfortable saying I can walk into a modernization engagement and use these tools productively on day one."*

**Q11. "You've managed teams of 2–3 analysts. What did that look like?"**
Keep it concrete — Mobis PoC delivery team + Plug and Play junior analysts. See the STAR doc for the full answer; for HR, a 60-second version is enough.

**Q12. "Your background is automotive and mobility. How does that translate to modernization and cyber work?"**
*"Three transferable threads. First, the actual craft — at Ford I owned non-functional requirements, SLA monitoring, and incident response for connected-vehicle services, which is the same muscle as modernization security posture and risk assessment. Second, the consulting delivery model — at Plug and Play and Mobis I ran multi-stakeholder innovation programs that look structurally identical to a TST engagement: diagnose current state, design future state, execute with a small team. Third, the AI angle — my NAV CANADA work was deliberately cross-sector, specifically applying frontier LLMs and MCP to legacy workflow assessment. So the domain shifts, but the delivery pattern is the same one I've run for years."*

**Q13. "You completed your MSIS in December 2025 and your last role ended in August 2025. What have you been doing since?"**
Direct, no apologizing.
*"Two things. One, finishing my MSIS through December 2025 — the last semester was heavy course-wise. Two, actively upskilling on the frontier AI stack — MCP, Claude Code, Copilot, vibe coding workflows in JavaScript and Java — because I decided I wanted my next role to be AI-modernization-focused and I wanted to walk into it prepared. I've been deliberate about targeting roles like this one rather than taking the first consulting offer that came."*

**Q14. "Why did you leave each of your previous roles?"**
Have a one-sentence answer for each. No negativity about past employers.
- **Ford → NavInfo**: Wanted to move from operational SME work into strategy and business development; NavInfo offered that at a China-market connected vehicle platform.
- **NavInfo → Plug and Play**: Wanted broader exposure to the global startup ecosystem and client-facing consulting; Plug and Play's venture arm was a direct step into that.
- **Plug and Play → Mobis Ventures**: Wanted to own a full innovation program end-to-end with investment authority, not just advise clients; Mobis offered that mandate.
- **Mobis → MSIS + NAV CANADA**: Chose to return to school for MSIS to formalize the technical foundation, relocated to Canada, and took the NAV CANADA co-op to anchor into the Canadian federal sector.

### Block D — Surface-level technical comfort check

HR is **not** going to ask you to explain MCP architecture. But they may ask:

**Q15. "On a scale of 1–10, how comfortable are you with AI coding tools?"**
*"Seven, trending up fast. I'm fluent with Claude Sonnet and Opus, Copilot, and MCP server setup at the practitioner level — I use them daily. What keeps me from saying a 9 is that my production software engineering experience is narrower than my strategy and analysis background; I'm closing that gap deliberately, but I won't overstate it."*

**Q16. "Explain in plain English what MCP is."**
*"MCP is a standard, introduced by Anthropic in late 2024, that lets AI models securely reach into your systems — your code repo, your Jira, your database — and act on them, not just talk about them. Without a standard like MCP, every AI integration is custom and ungovernable; with it, enterprise clients can let AI do real work with auditable access controls."*

**Q17. "How do you use AI tools in your current work?"**
*"Every day across three buckets. One, analytical research — Claude and ChatGPT for synthesizing dense source material and stress-testing arguments. Two, writing and coding — Copilot for scaffolding, Claude Code for multi-file refactors and small services. Three, orchestration — MCP servers to let an LLM pull from my own document and code repositories. The pattern I've settled into is: AI drafts, I steer, tests catch problems, humans own the final decision."*

## 3. "Why Consulting" — full answers

This is the question most candidates fumble. Have three distinct answers depending on how they phrase it.

### Version A — "Why consulting (in general)?"

*"Three reasons, honestly. First, variety — I've been at my best when I can move across problems and industries rather than optimize one thing for five years. My Ford-to-NavInfo-to-Plug-and-Play-to-Mobis path is essentially a consulting pattern already: diagnose the problem, design the answer, get it adopted, move on. Second, leverage — consulting is structurally designed so that your best work gets compounded across multiple clients instead of sitting inside one organization. Third, proximity to decisions — consultants get to work with executives on problems that actually matter to the business, and you see the direct link between analysis and action. Those three things are why I'm choosing consulting deliberately, not as a fallback."*

### Version B — "Why consulting now, after 7 years in industry?"

*"My industry roles were quasi-consulting in structure — at Plug and Play I was literally running client engagements, and at Mobis Ventures I was serving multiple internal stakeholders like clients. What industry gave me was the rigor of actually owning operational outcomes at Ford and investment decisions at Mobis — which makes my consulting recommendations more credible because I've lived on the receiving end of bad ones. Now, with the MSIS done and the frontier AI skill layer built, moving into formal consulting at a firm like EY is the step that compounds everything I've built rather than restarting in any one of the pieces."*

### Version C — "Why EY specifically vs. other Big 4?"

*"Three specific reasons for EY. One, the TST practice — the explicit combination of transaction strategy and transformation execution is distinctive; most firms separate those teams. Two, the federal book — my NAV CANADA experience and clearance eligibility line up directly, and EY's federal practice is serious. Three, what I've seen publicly about how EY is adopting frontier AI — MCP, Copilot, agentic workflows — tells me this is a firm that actually lets consultants build with the tools rather than just brief clients on them. That matters to me because the skill I'm bringing is hands-on, and I want to keep it that way."*

## 5. Salary & logistics — scripts

### Salary script (the most common HR misstep)

**Don't name a number first if you can help it.** Pivot to a range, tied to role level + market.

*"I'd want to get aligned on the role level and total compensation structure before pinning a number. For a role at this level in the TST practice, my expectation is the competitive market range — I've seen publicly reported bands for senior consultant / staff consultant roles at EY Canada and I'd expect to be inside those. Happy to share a concrete range once I understand how the role is leveled."*

**If pushed to name a range:** Research EY Staff / Senior Consultant Canada bands on Glassdoor, LinkedIn Salary, Levels.fyi before the call. Give a range ±15% around market midpoint. **Do not anchor low.**

**If asked current salary:** *"I'd prefer to talk about the target rather than my prior comp, since roles vary in scope — but I'm confident my expectation is consistent with market for this level."*

### Start date script
*"I can start immediately. I'd want to give any overlapping commitments appropriate notice, but for practical purposes I'm available on short notice."*

### Travel & relocation script
*"I'm based between Ottawa and Toronto, which gives flexibility on office location. I'm open to client-site travel and to office-based work. Full relocation would be a bigger conversation, but short-term mobility for engagements isn't a barrier."*

## 7. Red flags HR specifically watches for — how to avoid them

| Red flag | What it looks like | How to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Not actually interested | Generic "why EY" answer, can't describe the TST team specifically | Use the tailored Why-EY in §2/§3; mention TST by name multiple times |
| Communication issues | Rambling, filler, avoiding the question | Practice the 60-second open aloud; pause before answering instead of filling |
| Salary misalignment | Wildly off-market ask | Research before the call; use the pivot script in §5 |
| Clearance uncertainty | Hedging on eligibility | Be unambiguous: yes or no, with a sentence of context |
| Job-hopping concern | NavInfo was only 9 months | Have a positive forward-looking reason, never bad-mouth |
| Gap since Aug 2025 | Can't articulate what you've been doing | Use the answer in Q13 — finishing MSIS + deliberate upskilling |
| Over-claiming tech skills | Resume shows more hands-on dev than you can actually defend | Have the honest-but-confident framing in Q10, Q15, Q17 |
| Not interested in travel / on-site | Pushing back on client-site work | Affirm willingness in Q6; any specific constraints can be raised later |

## 10. The single most important thing

**The HR screen is mostly about whether they want to forward you to the next round. They rarely hire out of this call — they filter.** That means:

- Be warm, clear, and specific.
- Match their energy; don't over-lecture.
- Close with enthusiasm + a clear ask about next steps.
- Any answer you're not sure about: pause, think, then answer. Ten seconds of silence beats ninety seconds of rambling.

Good luck.
